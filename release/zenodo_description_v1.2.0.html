A hand-curated set of 21 protein regions across 11 proteins, shown experimentally to be required for, or to constitute the core of, protein aggregation. Every row was established by opening the source paper and checking the claim against it.

The dataset carries four columns that aggregation datasets usually lack, each because omitting it produces a benchmark that silently measures the wrong thing:

  • construct — which construct established the region. A region can be necessary in one construct and dispensable in another, and both results can be correct.
  • circularity_status — whether a predictor was in the discovery path. A region found by a predictor cannot be used to test that predictor. Circular rows are kept and labelled, not dropped.
  • stratum — whether experiment and prediction agree. Regions that are mapped cleanly tend to be the ones predictors find, so a dataset of well-mapped regions is enriched for agreement; only discordant rows can discriminate between predictors.
  • cpad_status — new in 1.1.0. Whether the row's protein is in CPAD 2.0, and therefore whether a modern predictor's hit on it may be recall of training data rather than prediction. This is a third kind of circularity, distinct from the discovery path and from experiment–prediction agreement.

The contamination check was the main change in 1.1.0, and its result is sobering. All 21 rows now carry it — the original 17 in 1.1.0, the four added in 1.2.0 as they were added — each checked against all three CPAD 2.0 datasets, read complete, plus an orthologue check in both OMA and OrthoInspector, because the CPAD90 training set transfers annotations to homologues. Six of the eleven proteins are CPAD members. Of the five rows in the discordant stratum — the only stratum that can discriminate between predictors — three are CPAD-contaminated and two are not. A reader benchmarking a modern predictor on this dataset without that column would measure memorisation on most of the rows that appear to matter.

New in 1.2.0: four rows, and a negative result about this dataset's own growth. Added are ANXA11 39-74, TMEM106B 120-254, glycinin G4 164-190 — the first non-animal row, a plant seed storage protein — and a DISC1 interval split into the two sub-intervals its source actually tested. The dataset grew from 17 rows to 21 and the usable discordant stratum did not move off 2 rows. That is worth stating plainly for anyone planning to extend it: row count is not the scarce resource. A row can only enter the discordant stratum if its source paper ran an aggregation predictor on the region and reported it disagreeing with the experiment, and most structural papers never run one at all — which is why 12 of 21 rows carry stratum: PENDING rather than a verdict. Extending this dataset usefully means searching methods sections for reported predictor failures, not searching for more proteins with mapped cores.

A caution on re-scoring, new in 1.2.0. Every row carries a frame, and the frame is not a formality: scoring a region inside a frame that contains a hydrophobic segment which was never in the sample — a signal peptide, a transmembrane helix — makes every predictor look worse, because such a segment scores highly and is right to. Glycinin G4 is the worked example: its precursor carries a 23-residue signal peptide, so the row is framed on the mature A4 chain that actually aggregated. Re-scoring these rows in a different frame will give different numbers for that reason and no other.

Limitations are stated in the data descriptor and should be read before use. The dataset is small and is not a random sample. After excluding CPAD-contaminated rows, the usable discordant stratum holds 2 rows, which supports a case series rather than a rate. 12 of 21 rows are not yet assessed for stratum. Rows are not independent. Most rows are recombinant or cell-based and cannot speak to aggregation in patients. The CPAD90 membership list is unpublished, so no row can be certified training-set-clean in absolute terms.
