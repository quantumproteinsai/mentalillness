# Tarone 1990, read in full — and our citation of it is mis-attributed

**[READ] Tarone RE (1990), "A Modified Bonferroni Method for Discrete Data", *Biometrics*
**46**(2):515–522, JSTOR 2531456. Publisher PDF supplied by the author, 20 Nov 2026, read
in full including both worked examples and the reference list.**

Read because, after the §10d withdrawal, **this project's sole surviving contribution is
defined by contrast with this paper** — "the bound is Tarone's rule; what is new is the
application." The register had it graded `[SNIPPET — Tarone itself NOT opened]`, while five
documents cited it flatly, one of them a deposited Zenodo description. That is the rule-1
line, and it was crossed.

---

## The finding: the exclusion rule is NOT Tarone 1990

**What §3.1b does** — compute the minimum attainable significance level for a hypothesis,
and set aside hypotheses that cannot reject at α — **is attributed by Tarone himself to
earlier work, in his own introduction.** Verbatim, p. 515:

> *"Gart, Chu, and Tarone (1979) noted that the number of significance tests could be
> reduced by eliminating those tests for which rejection was not possible at the nominal
> false positive rate, α, that is, for which α\* > α [see also Mantel (1980), in remarks
> attributed to John Tukey]."*

And again on p. 517:

> *"As a first step, Gart et al. (1979) and Mantel (1980) have noted that the Bonferroni
> correction factor can be reduced from I to m(1)."*

**So the correct citation for the exclusion is:**

- **Gart JJ, Chu KC, Tarone RE (1979)**, *J Natl Cancer Inst* **62**:957–974
- **Mantel N (1980)**, *Biometrics* **36**:381–399 — where the remark is attributed to
  **John Tukey**

Tarone 1990 is eleven years later and is not where the idea is introduced.

## What Tarone 1990 actually contributes, and we do not use it

Its own novelty, stated in its introduction as *"further considerations related to the
minimum achievable significance level,"* is a **sharper multiplicity correction factor**:

> For each integer *k*, let *m*(*k*) be the number of sites with *k*·α\*ᵢ ≤ α. Define **K
> as the smallest *k* with *m*(*k*)/*k* ≤ 1**, and reject at site *i* only if *i* ∈ *R_K*
> and *Pᵢ* < α/*K*.

In his chloroform example this takes the correction factor from *m*(1) = 23 down to
**K = 14**, which turns a non-significant male-rat kidney result significant. **That is a
Bonferroni refinement across many simultaneous tests.** This project performs no
Bonferroni correction across rows — the confirmatory statistic is a Stouffer combination —
so *Tarone's actual method is not in use here at all.*

**We were citing the paper for the one part of it that is not its own.**

## Why the mis-attribution happened, which is the transferable part

The citation came from a prior-art audit that graded it `[SNIPPET]` honestly: *"cited in
Sugiyama et al. arXiv:1407.0316, which was READ, and in the CRAN MHTdiscrete manual."*
Both are **significant-pattern-mining** sources, where **"Tarone's rule" is the community
name for the whole bound-and-exclude-and-correct package**, Tarone 1990 being the canonical
citation. That name is standard in *that* literature and is not wrong there. **It is wrong
as an attribution of the idea**, and reading the primary is the only thing that would have
shown it — the secondary sources say "Tarone's rule" and stop.

> **A SNIPPET gave the right paper for the wrong reason.** The register said so, in those
> words, and five documents cited it anyway — including one that is now under a DOI.

## What this does to the project's surviving claim

**It strengthens it slightly, and sharpens it a lot.**

- **The attribution is corrected** to Gart/Chu/Tarone 1979 and Mantel 1980 (Tukey), with
  Tarone 1990 cited as the standard reference for the refinement and for the name the
  pattern-mining literature uses.
- **The claimed novelty narrows and is better defined.** Our *use* differs from theirs in a
  way worth stating: Gart, Mantel and Tarone exclude untestable hypotheses **to shrink a
  multiplicity correction** and preserve family-wise error across many simultaneous tests.
  §3.1b excludes them **to say a benchmark row is uninformative in principle** — a
  statement about one row's design, applied at recruitment, before a paper is read in full.
  Same arithmetic, different purpose, and the second is what we claim.
- **It is still empty in this field.** Nothing found applies any of this to
  aggregation-predictor benchmarking.

## An opportunity this reading creates, NOT taken here

**Tarone's K is directly computable on our rows and we have never computed it.** With
α\*ᵢ = *m*/*M* per row, *K* is the smallest *k* with *m*(*k*)/*k* ≤ 1 over the 23 scored
rows. Because most of our rows have α\* > 0.05 and drop out at *k* = 1, **K is likely far
below 23**, which is the same qualitative point §4.2 makes — and it would let the benchmark
report a *family-wise* statement across rows that it currently cannot.

**Not computed here, deliberately.** It is a new analysis on unblinded data, and this
project's rule is that the statistic is written down first. If it is wanted, it is
registered first.
